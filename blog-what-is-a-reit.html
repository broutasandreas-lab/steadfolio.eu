What Is a REIT? Real Estate Investing for Beginners

4 min read - Educational content, not financial advice

Real estate has long had a reputation as a solid, tangible investment - but buying property directly requires large amounts of capital, ongoing management, and a level of hands-on commitment most beginners aren't looking for. REITs exist to solve exactly that problem.

What a REIT actually is

A REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust) is a company that owns, and usually operates, income-producing real estate - things like apartment buildings, office towers, shopping centers, warehouses, or data centers. When you buy a share of a publicly traded REIT, you're buying a small stake in that portfolio of properties, without ever having to buy, manage, or maintain a building yourself.

Why REITs are known for higher dividend yields

Most REITs are legally required to distribute the large majority of their taxable income - often 90% or more, depending on the jurisdiction - directly to shareholders as dividends. This structural requirement is why REITs, as a category, tend to have noticeably higher dividend yields than typical stocks. In exchange, REITs generally retain less cash for their own growth than other companies would.

Different types of REITs

  • Equity REITs: Own and operate physical properties directly, earning income primarily through rent - the most common type.
  • Mortgage REITs: Don't own property directly, but instead finance real estate by holding mortgages or mortgage-backed securities, earning income from interest.
  • Sector-focused REITs: Specialize in a specific property type - residential, industrial, healthcare, data centers - rather than a broad mix.

What actually drives a REIT's performance?

REIT prices and payouts tend to be sensitive to a few specific factors that don't affect ordinary stocks in quite the same way:

  • Interest rates - REITs often borrow heavily to finance property purchases, so rising rates can increase their costs and make their dividend yields comparatively less attractive versus other income options.
  • Occupancy rates - how full the underlying properties are directly affects rental income.
  • Sector-specific trends - a REIT focused on office space, for example, faces different pressures than one focused on warehouses or data centers.
Worth remembering: A high REIT dividend yield isn't automatically a bargain - it's worth understanding what's driving it, the same way it's worth checking with any high-yielding investment.

How to access REITs

Publicly traded REITs are bought and sold on stock exchanges exactly like ordinary shares, through a standard brokerage account - no special account type needed. Broad REIT-focused ETFs also exist, giving you diversified exposure to many REITs at once rather than betting on a single company or sector, similar in spirit to how a broad stock ETF diversifies across many companies. Our guide on how to evaluate an ETF applies just as well when you're looking at a REIT-focused fund.

Where REITs fit in a broader portfolio

REITs are sometimes described as a distinct asset class alongside stocks and bonds, since real estate doesn't always move in the same direction as the broader stock market. Whether - and how much - to include them is a personal decision tied to your goals and overall diversification, not a requirement for every investor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a REIT in simple terms?

A REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust) is a company that owns and typically operates income-producing real estate, such as apartments, offices, or warehouses. Buying a share of a REIT lets you invest in real estate without buying or managing property yourself.

Do REITs pay dividends?

Most REITs are legally required to distribute a large majority of their taxable income to shareholders as dividends, which is why they're often known for relatively high dividend yields compared to typical stocks.

Are REITs risky?

REITs carry their own risks, including sensitivity to interest rate changes and the health of the real estate sector they focus on. They shouldn't be treated as a risk-free substitute for cash, even though many offer relatively high dividend yields.

Can I buy a REIT the same way I buy a stock?

Publicly traded REITs are bought and sold on stock exchanges just like ordinary shares, through a regular brokerage account. Non-traded REITs also exist but work quite differently and are generally less accessible and less liquid.
