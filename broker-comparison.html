How to read this comparison

The cheapest broker on paper isn't always the cheapest for you. What you'll actually pay depends on how you invest:

  • Small monthly amounts: a fixed fee weighs heavily on small orders. €1 is 2% of a €50 purchase but 0.1% of €1,000. Free savings plans or €0-commission orders matter most here.
  • Mainly ETFs in euros: a UCITS ETF bought in euros on a European exchange needs no currency conversion at the broker, so the FX fee matters less than the ETF commission.
  • Shares priced in US dollars: the FX fee applies when you buy and again when you sell, so it can cost more than the commission.
  • Your country: availability, products and tax reporting differ by country. Check the broker supports where you live before comparing anything else.
  • Protection: look at who regulates the broker and how client assets and cash are held. EU deposit guarantees and investor compensation schemes cover different things.

Frequently asked questions

Which broker has the lowest fees for ETFs?

It depends on how you invest. Trade Republic and Lightyear charge €0 commission on ETF orders (Trade Republic adds a small settlement fee outside savings plans). DEGIRO charges €1 on ETFs within its Core Selection on Tradegate. The lowest-cost option depends on your exchange, order size, and whether you use a savings plan.

What is the best broker for beginners in Europe?

There is no single best broker - it depends on your country, how much you plan to invest, and whether you want fractional shares or automatic savings plans. This comparison lays out the actual fees and features of 10 popular brokers so you can compare them yourself.

Is Trading 212 or DEGIRO cheaper?

For most retail investors buying stocks and ETFs, Trading 212 charges €0 commission plus a 0.15% FX fee, while DEGIRO charges €1-3 per ETF trade depending on the exchange, plus a 0.25% FX fee. Which is cheaper overall depends on your trade size and frequency - check the full comparison table for details.
